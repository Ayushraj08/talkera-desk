**Motion: alive, not noisy.** The full rules are in C1. Everything stops under reduced motion.

**Responsive (100%).** Mobile first. Test at 360, 390, 768, 1024, 1280 and 1536px wide. No sideways scrolling anywhere. Tap targets at least 44px. Pricing tables turn into stacked cards on phones. Test on a real small iPhone and a mid-range Android.

**Performance.** Static pages (Astro, Eleventy or Next static export) on Cloudflare Pages. Google's "good" thresholds: LCP under 2.5s, INP under 200ms, CLS under 0.1. Keep JavaScript under about 100KB. Images in AVIF or WebP with width and height set. Lighthouse mobile 90 or higher.

**Accessibility.** Visible focus states, keyboard navigation, alt text, a visible transcript beside every audio file, captions on video, labelled form fields.

## 7. SEO, GEO and AEO

**What I found.** Most AEO and GEO advice online comes from agencies selling services. A recent analysis argues that llms.txt and schema markup have no measurable effect on AI citations: it cites an Ahrefs live test showing under 5% movement either way, Google stating that Search ignores llms.txt, and a log study where 97% of llms.txt files received zero requests in May 2026 (capitalandcompute.net, 6 days old). Other guides claim big gains, and I could not verify those. Treat the following split as my recommendation, not settled fact.

**Do first (basic, proven or harmless):**

- Static, fast, mobile-friendly HTML. AI and search crawlers read the page without running scripts.
- One H1 per page, unique title under 60 characters, description under 155, clean lowercase hyphenated URLs, canonical tags, XML sitemap, internal links from every page to Pricing and Demo.
- Open every section with a direct answer of 40 to 60 words under a question-style heading. Several sources recommend this for AI extraction. It also helps people.
- Put one quotable, dated, numeric sentence near the top of key pages (for example the pricing line on /pricing). Keep it updated.
- Keep facts identical everywhere: name, description, prices, location. Inconsistent facts make AI answers wrong.
- robots.txt: allow Googlebot, Bingbot, OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, Claude-SearchBot and PerplexityBot. Allowing training crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended) is your call; for a new brand wanting visibility I would allow them.
- Rank in classic search first. One source says about 87% of ChatGPT citations overlap Bing's top 10, and another says 38% of AI Overview citations come from Google's top 10. Both are single-source, so use them as direction only.
- Link to or cite sources for every number. Show author and updated date on guides.

**Do, but do not expect AI magic:**

- JSON-LD schema: Organization (with sameAs for your real social profiles), WebSite, Service on each vertical page, Offer for plans, BreadcrumbList, Article on guides, FAQPage on /faq. It keeps machines' data clean. Google no longer shows FAQ rich results for most sites, so the value is clarity, not a snippet.
- llms.txt: optional, ten minutes of work, no proven effect. Last priority.

**Off-site (this is what moves AI and search visibility for a new brand):**

- A complete LinkedIn company page and a YouTube channel with the demo calls (with transcripts).
- Be mentioned by others: posts and comments in contractor groups, guest posts, directory listings (Crunchbase now, G2 or Capterra later). Create no fake reviews.
- Case studies as soon as a client allows it.

**Keywords to target (validate volumes with free tools before committing):** AI receptionist for restoration companies, after-hours answering for water damage companies, AI answering service for plumbers, AI call answering for HVAC. Match each phrase to one page. Do not write programmatic city pages.

**Tracking.** Events: demo call tap, audio play, calculator use, form submit, calendar booking. Review weekly.

## D0. What kind of website works best here

A **product-led, proof-first, editorial site**. Not a brochure and not a template landing page. Three rules decide it:

1. **The product is the hero.** The visitor must see a call being answered, a job booked and a text sent within five seconds, built as real on-page components (phone screens, a dashboard, a transcript), not screenshots and not stock art.
2. **One bold idea per screen.** Huge type, short lines, one visual, one action. Clay, Ramp and Tines all do this. Everything else is cut.
3. **Craft is the trust signal.** For a buyer who has never heard of you, polish reads as "established". Spacing, type, easing curves and tiny details are what make someone say "who built this?".

Honest limit: an AI coding agent can build the layout, the motion and the code well. It cannot invent a distinctive illustration style or taste. Those two things (the object illustrations in D3 and your copy edit pass) are where you spend your own effort, or a freelancer's.
